# Root License Decision

No root license has been selected for workspace-owned code. Until the owner makes and records a decision, no permission to copy, modify, or redistribute that code should be inferred. Existing third-party license files apply only to their covered sources; see `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md`.

## Reasonable Options

### Apache License 2.0

Permissive reuse with an explicit patent grant and notice requirements. This can suit research software intended for broad academic and commercial use, but the owner should confirm that all workspace-owned contributions and dependencies are compatible.

### MIT License

Short, permissive terms with broad reuse rights and warranty disclaimer. It is simpler than Apache-2.0 but does not provide the same explicit patent language.

### GPLv3

Strong copyleft requiring distributed derivative works to remain under compatible open terms. This can preserve downstream openness but may limit integration with some projects and deployment models.

### Research-only or custom terms

Can restrict use to research or impose additional conditions, but custom terms create compatibility and interpretation costs. They should be reviewed by qualified counsel before adoption.

### No root license for now

Retains default copyright restrictions while the research and third-party provenance are clarified. Public visibility alone does not grant reuse rights.

## Owner Checklist

- Identify which files are workspace-owned versus copied or modified upstream code.
- Confirm contributor rights and third-party license compatibility.
- Decide whether commercial use, patent rights, and reciprocal sharing are desired.
- Add the chosen root license and update `THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md` only after that decision.
